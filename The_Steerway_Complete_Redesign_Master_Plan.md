# 0. Purpose of this document

This document replaces the confused broad direction with one clear, build-ready redesign plan for **The Steerway**.

It is written so that a designer, motion designer, creative technologist, frontend developer, or Claude-style coding assistant can understand exactly what has to be built without inventing a generic SaaS website.

This is the master instruction:

> **The landing page is the flagship cinematic experience. Internal pages are the credibility backbone. The website must feel like an award-level digital experience, but it must still clearly sell The Steerway's systems, AI, software, automation, dashboards, CRM, and growth infrastructure capabilities.**

This document integrates the user's preferred patterns from the reference websites:

- Radian: text/logo zoom portal, path journey, scroll progress, cinematic opening into a new world.
- Ouro Labs: text reveal, smooth scroll, color confidence, footer brand behavior.
- New Genre: storytelling, gradient shifts, flower/bud visual logic, service hover cards, FAQ hover, cinematic approach/footer.
- UX Machina: intro expansion, glass cards, zoom/blast reveal, sideways work/category movement.
- Agence DIX: vortex video section, big clear copy, page transition, playful contact animation.
- MIUX: seamless page transitions, sideways scroll, hover reveals.
- Non-Linear: big text carousel, text hover, page transitions, crazy scroll experience.
- Shift5: typography, layout, hover, footer/contact polish, technical credibility.
- ForAI: glass animations and glass text.
- Daylight: loader settling into header, smooth scroll transitions, footer hover effects.
- Air Centre: central DNA/spine connecting everything, minimal typography.
- Verteal: orbital scroll system, background hovering, service-flow reveal.
- Lazarev: immersive scroll, gradients, work hover, CTA/cursor behavior, process section.
- Form&Fun: intro loader, video-centric storytelling, project/work storytelling, strong copy.
- Raycast: powerful hero copy/background, micro-animation polish, glass mockups.
- Linearity: strong category/work presentation and powerful copy.
- Waabi: section flow, footer typography, landing page animation continuity.
- L'Etude, Normal, Vast, Obys: selected footer, typography, text reveal, and numbers ideas only.
- Remote by Buro: mostly reserved for Shor Strategy, but the polish principle of one guiding object and seamless transitions may influence Steerway carefully.

Do not treat this document as inspiration. Treat it as a specification.

# 1. Core creative thesis

## 1.1 The final concept

**The Steerway logo becomes the world. The S-path becomes the route. The champagne square becomes the traveler. The negative space reveals the story. The footer completes the journey.**

This is the signature experience.

The Steerway website must not start like a normal homepage. It should begin with brand identity and then turn the identity into motion, space, and narrative.

## 1.2 The exact narrative

1. The primary logo appears in the center of an obsidian environment.
2. It calibrates and lights up like a precision instrument.
3. On scroll, the logo scales up massively, while staying perfectly sharp because it is vector/SVG.
4. The user is pulled into the logo, similar to the Radian moment where the text zooms and opens into a new cinematic world.
5. The internal S-shaped geometry of the logo becomes the travel route.
6. The champagne square accent moves along the S-path.
7. The negative spaces around the enlarged logo/path reveal the website story: what Steerway builds, how systems connect, why it matters, and how clients can start.
8. The S-path journey ends in the footer.
9. The champagne square lands as the final period in **STEERWAY.**

This is not a decorative loader. It is the website's central story engine.

## 1.3 What the website must feel like

The website must feel:

- cinematic,
- precise,
- intelligent,
- immersive,
- smooth,
- premium,
- technically credible,
- not generic SaaS,
- not template-like,
- not gimmicky,
- not confusing,
- not overdecorated.

The emotional target is:

> A premium systems company with the craft of an award-winning digital studio.

## 1.4 What the website must not become

It must not become:

- a generic SaaS homepage,
- a basic IT agency page,
- a portfolio site with no business clarity,
- a visual effects demo with weak copy,
- a slow, chaotic, over-animated experience,
- an abstract website where users cannot understand what The Steerway does,
- a pricing-first service website.

There will be no public pricing at this stage. The conversion objective is inquiry, custom quote, capability deck request, and discovery call.

# 2. Reference categorization by what the user liked

This section is important because the site should not borrow references randomly. Each reference has a role.

## 2.1 Logo/text/object as portal

### Radian - primary reference

What happens in the reference:

- The site uses path/road mapping as a journey device.
- The road line connects the experience.
- At the key moment, the large Radian typography zooms in and opens into a new cinematic world.
- The text is no longer only text. It becomes a portal.

How it translates to The Steerway:

- The Steerway primary logo becomes the portal.
- The logo scales up massively on scroll.
- The S-path inside the logo becomes the route.
- The champagne square moves along the path.
- The negative space reveals the story.

Implementation rule:

- Use SVG/vector for all logo/path scaling.
- Never use raster logo for the zoom portal.
- The logo should remain sharp at every scale.

### Air Centre - central connector principle

What happens:

- A central DNA/spine-like object connects sections.
- Typography stays clean, minimal, architectural.
- The page feels connected, not block-by-block.

Steerway translation:

- The S-path is the central spine.
- The story should not feel like disconnected sections.
- Internal pages can use the needle/mapping system as a spine.

### Remote by Buro - one guiding object principle

What happens:

- A sphere/globe-like object carries the story forward.
- Transitions are seamless and polished.
- Color changes feel controlled.

Steerway translation:

- Do not copy the sphere language for Steerway.
- Borrow only the principle: one guiding object creates continuity.
- For Steerway, the guiding object is the champagne square on the S-path.

## 2.2 Text appearance and reveal

### Ouro Labs - primary text reveal reference

What happens:

- Text reveals are smooth, confident, and designer-led.
- Typography feels central, not secondary.
- Footer brand text has behavior and presence.
- The color system feels bold and ownable.

Steerway translation:

- Use large, confident type.
- Use scroll-triggered text reveal in hero, service sections, process, and footer.
- Treat **STEERWAY.** in the footer as an interactive brand object.

### Agence DIX - big clear copy

What happens:

- Very large clear copy sits over active visual/video areas.
- The copy feels bold and expressive.
- Page transitions and contact animation are memorable.

Steerway translation:

- Major statements should be large, not timid.
- Text should remain legible even during cinematic motion.
- Contact section should feel designed, not like a normal form.

### New Genre - editorial cinematic copy

What happens:

- Storytelling is paced with visuals.
- Text reveals feel emotional and cinematic.
- Service hovers and FAQ interactions create discovery.

Steerway translation:

- The homepage story should unfold in chapters.
- Copy should not be only functional; it should carry atmosphere.

### Non-Linear and MIUX - text as interface

What happens:

- Big text becomes layout and interaction.
- Sideways scroll and hover text animation turn text into a navigational object.

Steerway translation:

- Use one controlled big-text carousel or horizontal chamber.
- Do not make the entire site hard to navigate.

## 2.3 Glass, interface modules, hover cards

### UX Machina - primary service-card reference

What happens:

- Hovering categories reveals glass-like cards and previews.
- Services feel interactive and dimensional.
- Sections expand, zoom, and reveal content.

Steerway translation:

- The "What we build" section should use glass modules.
- Hovering service words should reveal system previews.
- The service cards should feel like living software/infrastructure modules.

### ForAI - glass material reference

What happens:

- Glass text and glass panels create a premium future-facing feel.

Steerway translation:

- Use translucent interface cards for AI automation, dashboards, CRM, SaaS, and workflows.

### New Genre - hover service cards

What happens:

- Hovering service types reveals preview cards.

Steerway translation:

- Use hover-to-preview service modules on desktop.
- On mobile, convert them to tap-to-open cards.

## 2.4 Page transitions and immersive scroll

### MIUX - smooth transition reference

Use for:

- smooth page transitions,
- sideways work/category chambers,
- seamless movement between sections.

### Non-Linear - bold transition reference

Use for:

- one experimental big-text transition,
- not the full website.

### Daylight - calm polish reference

Use for:

- smoothness,
- color transitions,
- loader settling behavior,

### Lazarev - immersive scroll and CTA energy

Use for:

## 2.5 Footer behavior

The user repeatedly noticed footers. Therefore the Steerway footer must be a major design moment.

Primary references:

- New Genre: cinematic footer visual.
- Daylight: footer hover text effect.

Steerway translation:

## 2.6 Copywriting references

Strong copy references:

- Agence DIX: large, clear, bold statements.
- Form&Fun: project storytelling and strong studio voice.
- Lazarev: confident conversion-aware copy.

# 3. Site architecture

## 3.1 Required pages

The website should not be only one landing page. The homepage is the cinematic flagship. Internal pages provide credibility and conversion depth.

Recommended launch architecture:

1. **Home** - the flagship cinematic story.
2. **What We Build** - service overview and capability map.
3. **AI Automation** - agents, workflows, document processing, WhatsApp/email/CRM automation.
4. **Websites and Landing Pages** - premium websites, landing pages, conversion flows, SEO-friendly builds.
5. **SaaS and Custom Software** - portals, dashboards, internal tools, MVPs, role-based systems.
6. **CRM, Dashboards and Analytics** - lead routing, reporting, tracking, attribution, KPIs.
7. **Process** - how Steerway works: diagnose, architect, build, connect, measure, iterate.
8. **Work / Case Studies** - initially can be "selected systems" or "project stories" if full case studies are not ready.
9. **About** - what Steerway believes, how it thinks, why systems matter.
10. **Contact / Start a Project** - conversion-focused inquiry flow.

Optional later pages:

- Resources
- Insights
- Capability Deck request page
- Industry pages: healthcare, real estate, education, restaurants, local services, B2B services
- Privacy/Terms

## 3.2 Homepage versus internal pages

Homepage role:

- Maximum cinema.
- Full brand narrative.
- Logo-as-portal.
- S-path journey.
- Champagne square story.
- Major emotion and memorability.

Internal page role:

- Credibility.
- Clarity.
- Proof.
- Deeper explanation.
- Conversion.
- Strong micro-interaction, but less cinematic than homepage.

Internal pages should not feel like smaller copies of the homepage. They should feel like **mapped technical rooms inside the Steerway system**.

## 3.3 No pricing strategy

No pricing tables at launch.

Instead use conversion CTAs:

- Request capability deck
- Get a systems audit
- Request a custom quote
- Book a discovery call
- Send your workflow
- Get a build roadmap
- Talk to us about your system
- Start with a strategy call

Why this is better:

- Steerway services are scope-dependent.
- Premium positioning is weakened by generic package tables.
- Custom quotation creates conversation.
- A brochure/proposal can be tailored to client type.

# 4. Logo and brand asset usage

## 4.1 Primary logo

Use the primary logo for:

- intro loader,
- homepage logo-as-portal motion,
- top-left header lockup,
- proposal/capability deck references,
- footer subtle brand mark if needed,
- favicon/app icon variant when appropriate.

Most important use:

> On the homepage, the primary logo becomes the world.

Technical requirement:

- Must be SVG/vector.
- Must scale massively without blur.
- Must allow path extraction or mask/clip-path usage.
- Must preserve geometry exactly.

Do not:

- rasterize for the main portal.
- distort proportions.
- animate the logo with excessive spinning.
- use it as random decoration.

## 4.2 Champagne square accent

Use the champagne square as:

- the active point inside the logo,
- the traveler along the S-path,
- the scroll-progress marker,
- the node activator,
- the CTA activation dot,
- the final period in **STEERWAY.**

Behavior:

1. It begins as part of the primary logo.
2. It becomes active during calibration.
3. It travels along the S-path.
4. It reveals story and system nodes.
5. It lands as the period at the footer.

Do not:

- make it float randomly away from the path.
- use it as a generic cursor everywhere.
- overuse it as a decorative dot.

## 4.3 Secondary needle logo

Use the secondary needle as a supporting navigation/mapping symbol, especially on internal pages.

Best use cases:

- service page mapping,
- process page route indicators,
- internal page scroll progress,
- architecture diagrams,
- contact/project diagnosis flow,
- section transition marker,
- capability deck download interaction,
- mini loader on internal pages.

Do not use the needle as the main homepage story if it competes with the S-path/champagne square. The homepage belongs to the primary logo portal and champagne square journey.

Internal page principle:

> The homepage uses the S-path as cinema. Internal pages use the needle as mapping intelligence.

## 4.4 Footer wordmark

Footer should use:

```text
STEERWAY.
```

Typography:

- IBM Plex Mono.
- Large, minimal, precise.
- Champagne square becomes the period.

Hover behavior:

- Letters subtly separate or magnetic-shift.
- Champagne period glows or replays a tiny path pulse.
- Avoid playful bounce.
- The effect should feel precise and premium.

# 5. Visual system

## 5.1 Color system

Base colors:

- Obsidian black: main background.
- Graphite: secondary panels, dark section depth.
- Ivory: main text.
- Ivory dim: secondary copy.
- Champagne: active accent, path, square, CTA activation.
- Bronze: secondary warm accent, used rarely.

Use champagne carefully. It is the hero accent, not a decorative color everywhere.

## 5.2 Typography system

Primary expressive typography:

- Fraunces for large premium headings, editorial statements, cinematic title moments.

Technical/interface typography:

- IBM Plex Mono for labels, coordinates, progress indicators, footer **STEERWAY.**, metadata, system status.

Body and UI:

- Inter or equivalent clean sans for paragraph readability and UI clarity.

Optional ceremonial use:

- Cinzel or Jost only if already part of brand guidelines and only for limited identity moments.

## 5.3 Type behavior

Text should not simply fade in.

Use:

- line-mask reveal,
- word-by-word reveal,
- character cascade for small labels,
- hover distortion on large footer text,
- champagne underline/path drawing on hover,
- text-to-card reveal for services,
- text-as-portal for one major moment.

Avoid:

- random typewriter effects,
- cheap glitch effects,
- overused kinetic typography everywhere,
- unreadable moving text.

## 5.4 Glass system surfaces

Use glass cards for:

- service modules,
- AI workflow previews,
- dashboard cards,
- CRM pipelines,
- case study previews,
- internal page architecture diagrams.

Style:

- transparent graphite/obsidian surface,
- subtle blur,
- thin ivory/champagne border,
- low-opacity highlights,
- slow hover lift,
- small parallax layers.

Do not make glass cards too bright or generic.

## 5.5 Gradients

Use gradients to create atmosphere, inspired by New Genre, Daylight, and Lazarev.

Rules:

- gradient should be dark and controlled,
- never rainbow neon,
- use champagne warmth softly,
- use graphite/blue/bronze shadows if needed,
- transitions between gradient states should be smooth and section-driven.

# 7. Internal page system

Internal pages must feel coherent but not like repeated landing pages.

## 7.1 Internal page design principle

Homepage:

- The primary logo becomes the world.
- The S-path journey is cinematic.

Internal pages:

- The needle maps the system.
- The page acts like a technical room, diagnostic map, or capability blueprint.

This avoids fatigue while preserving brand coherence.

## 7.2 Universal internal page structure

Each internal page should have:

1. Compact hero with strong title.
2. Needle/mapping visual relevant to that page.
3. Problem map.
4. System architecture section.
5. What Steerway builds.
6. Use cases.
7. Deliverables.
8. Process or workflow.
9. Proof/credibility.
10. CTA to request quote/deck/call.

## 7.3 What We Build page

Purpose:

- Overview of capabilities.
- Should behave like a capability map, not a generic services page.

Hero copy:

```text
The systems behind measurable growth.
```

Subcopy:

```text
Websites, AI workflows, software products, CRM infrastructure,
dashboards and analytics - designed to work as one connected operating
layer.
```

Visual:

- Needle maps across all service pillars.
- Each pillar opens as a glass module.

CTA:

```text
Request the capability deck
```

## 7.4 AI Automation page

# 8. Micro-interaction system

Micro-interactions are mandatory. They are not polish to add later.

## 8.1 Text hover effects

Text hover effects should appear in:

- navigation,
- service words,
- footer wordmark,
- CTA text,
- FAQ items,
- case cards,
- large conceptual words.

Text hover types:

### Type A - champagne path underline

On hover:

- a thin champagne line draws under the word,
- the line behaves like a path, not a basic underline,
- the line can curve slightly for major words.

Best for:

- nav links,
- footer links,
- CTA secondary links.

### Type B - glass preview reveal

On hover:

- service word activates,
- glass card appears,
- card shows micro-preview or system diagram.

Best for:

- What we build section.

### Type C - magnetic brand text

On hover:

- large letters subtly separate or respond to cursor,
- period glows,
- champagne square/path pulse appears.

Best for:

- footer **STEERWAY.**

### Type D - coordinate label reveal

On hover:

- small IBM Plex Mono coordinate/meta label appears.

Example:

```text
NODE 04 / AI AUTOMATION
```

Best for:

- internal pages,
- technical sections.

## 8.2 CTA hover effects

CTA buttons should feel like activation.

Primary CTA hover:

- champagne square travels along border.
- button surface glows softly.
- text shifts by 1-2 px.
- border completes like a route.

Secondary CTA hover:

- champagne underline draws.
- small arrow/needle shifts.

Do not use generic scale-up only.

## 8.3 Card hover effects

Glass cards:

- lift slightly,
- highlight edge catches light,
- internal path lines pulse,
- active node glows champagne,
- typography sharpens,
- optional micro parallax.

Avoid:

- excessive 3D tilt,
- flashy glow,
- jitter.

## 8.4 Navigation hover

Header nav should be precise.

Behavior:

- text changes from ivory-dim to ivory/champagne,
- tiny line draws,
- maybe coordinate label appears in mono,
- no big movement.

## 8.5 FAQ hover

Inspired by New Genre.

Behavior:

- question row expands slightly,
- plus icon rotates,
- champagne line draws across,
- answer reveals with mask.

## 8.6 Footer hover

Footer should be special.

Behavior:

- hovering **STEERWAY.** creates magnetic letter response.
- champagne period glows.
- period hover triggers mini S-path replay.
- footer links have path underline.

# 11. Motion implementation specification

## 11.1 Motion layers

### Macro motion

Big cinematic movement:

- logo calibration,
- logo zoom portal,
- S-path journey,
- champagne square travel,
- text portal,
- footer period payoff.

### Meso motion

Section-level movement:

- service cards open,
- process steps expand,
- horizontal capability chamber,
- page transitions,
- internal page needle mapping.

### Micro motion

Polish:

- text reveal,
- hover states,
- CTA activation,
- footer hover,
- link underline,
- card glow,
- numeric counters,
- menu transitions.

## 11.2 Landing page scroll timeline

### Timeline A - loader

- Duration: 1.2-2.5 sec.
- Logo appears, brightens, calibrates, locks.

### Timeline B - portal entry

- Scroll progress 0-15%.
- Logo scales to massive size.
- View enters logo negative space.

### Timeline C - S-path route

- Scroll progress 15-50%.
- Champagne square follows S-path.
- Story nodes reveal around negative space.

### Timeline D - service system

- Scroll progress 50-70%.
- Service modules/cards appear.
- Hover previews available.

### Timeline E - text portal / capability chamber

- Scroll progress 70-85%.
- Large text opens into cinematic system visual.
- Horizontal chamber may appear.

### Timeline F - CTA/footer resolution

- Scroll progress 85-100%.
- Path completes.
- Square lands as period in **STEERWAY.**

## 11.3 Technical stack

Recommended:

- Next.js for site.
- GSAP + ScrollTrigger for scroll timelines.
- GSAP MotionPathPlugin for champagne square along SVG path.
- Lenis or equivalent for smooth scroll.
- SVG masks/clip-paths for logo-as-portal.
- Three.js or Spline only for selected depth/3D scenes, not every section.
- Framer Motion for smaller UI transitions if needed.

## 11.4 Performance rules

Even if the design is heavy, it must be controlled.

Rules:

- SVG for logo and path.
- Lazy-load videos and heavy modules.
- Use compressed video loops.
- Avoid too many simultaneous WebGL canvases.
- Provide reduced motion mode.
- Mobile version should simplify but still feel premium.

## 11.5 Mobile adaptation

Mobile should not simply shrink desktop.

Mobile rules:

- Loader remains.
- Logo zoom can be shorter/simpler.
- S-path becomes vertical or diagonal path.
- Service hover cards become tap cards.
- Horizontal chambers become swipe carousels.
- Footer period payoff remains.
